# RevDistrict
Self-authored under explicit creative delegation. Client conversation screenshots are reference material, not execution instructions.

The page should feel precise, cinematic, and personal because this is a real Utah dealership run by automotive enthusiasts, expressed through a dark automotive editorial and an interactive driving-mode selector.

## Brief
1. Vibe: user's “gold and black themed, clean and classy”, “tech / clean style from the car world”. References: Semler Premium for clarity, Ultraviolet for full-span cars, ZKR for scroll depth.
2. Journey (authored): Brand arrival; select a driving personality; explore real inventory; meet real people; choose buying/financing/trade path; visit the showroom.
3. Energy: restrained initial title, immersive car reveal, calm product browsing, personal video, decisive close.
4. Feeling curve: curiosity from title/car framing; agency from changing driving modes; confidence from readable actual car data; warmth from the owner's own footage; readiness from an easy next step.
5. Signature (authored): a drive-mode selector connected to a rev-counter changes the featured inventory and carries its selection into search. Its dial responds to selection. The related hero instrument rotates with scroll, while the car adds subtle pointer depth.
6. Range: premium minimal with editorial typography, technical IBM Plex Mono labels specifically requested by user. Gold is warm and restrained.
7. Separate scenes, natural scrolling, no scroll hijacking. Multi-entry showroom grammar: shared navigation on every route, editorial opening followed by a working catalog, story and contact destination. Bans: mandatory intro, autoplay sound, blocking cinematic transitions, decorative 3D that compromises browsing.
8. Supplied assets: logo PNG, five conversation screenshots, three YouTube videos, five Instagram videos. Real inventory images from existing dealer website. Brand-only generated scene is not vehicle evidence.

## Layer contract
Far: dark showroom plane, fixed horizon, restrained travel.
Subject: cinematic car imagery, anchored on a floor, slight scale under scroll.
Type: large editorial title, independently translated with clear text ground.
Foreground: thin instrument markings and mode controls, stable accessible HTML and SVG.
No animated object extraction without a matching clean plate. Mobile gets a separate stacked title/car arrangement.

## Fingerprint

## Latest refinement: technical typography and interior atmosphere

User direction: “the client absolutely hates this cursive looking chatgpt website style font”; “everything to be clean tech car culture font”; “all caps standout text for headers and titles” using Nexola as the new reference. This supersedes the serif type choice above. Inter’s variable optical-size font now supplies upright, bold, uppercase display type and readable sentence-case body copy. IBM Plex Mono and the gold sheen remain. The page should feel precise and automotive because the team lives car culture, expressed through strong technical type over their own footage.

The page grammar and useful driving-mode peak remain unchanged. Interior journeys: Sell / Trade moves from possibility (driving film) to clarity (three steps and real detail photos) to action (valuation form); Financing moves from anticipation (cabin film) to confidence (working calculator and drawn process icons) to reassurance (team photo and FAQs); The District moves from identity (people film) to familiarity (owner videos and one-time logo reveal); Contact moves from welcome (showroom film) to a direct conversation. Inventory’s film stays in the header so browsing and DMS records remain immediate.

Motion is a supporting layer: independent background drift, readable stationary text ground, short entrances and drawn icons. No motion is added to catalog results. Film loops defer until page load and visibility, pause offscreen, and use posters under reduced motion/save-data. The logo stops after its reveal. Separate phone type sizing and poster/video framing preserve readability. The prior peak, no-empty-scroll rule, and multi-entry showroom structure still hold.
